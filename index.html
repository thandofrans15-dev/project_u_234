02 — Chain Reaction Lab

Fission, controlled.

When a neutron splits a Uranium-235 atom, it releases heat and more neutrons — enough to split roughly one more atom on average, in a properly controlled reactor. Control rods absorb spare neutrons so the reaction stays steady instead of multiplying out of control.

03 — Passive Safety Simulator

What happens when the power goes out?

Older reactors needed working pumps and diesel generators to keep cooling the core after shutdown. Modern SMRs are designed around passive safety: gravity-fed water tanks and natural convection keep the core cool with zero electricity, zero human action, for days.

05 — A Global Safety Study

How small fission reactors stay safe, everywhere they're built.

SMRs aren't just a lab concept — versions are already running, under construction, or in active planning on nearly every continent, in climates from Arctic tundra to tropical coastline. The engineering changes site to site. The safety framework underneath doesn't. Spin the globe or pick a site to read more.

The framework

Four things every safe deployment shares.

◎

One Shared Rulebook

The International Atomic Energy Agency sets baseline safety standards that most national regulators build on, so a plant in Russia, China, or Canada is judged against a broadly similar bar — not a purely local one.

⛨

Defense-in-Depth, Everywhere

Fuel cladding, vessel, cooling backups, and containment — the same layered "if this fails, the next layer catches it" design shows up whether the reactor sits on a barge in the Arctic or a coastline in Hainan.

✦

Engineered for the Site

A floating plant is built to shrug off pack ice and swells. A desert design leans on air cooling over water. Small, factory-built reactors can be tuned to local climate and grid size in a way giant plants rarely can.

⬡

Fuel That's Hard to Divert

Most SMR fuel is low-enriched — far below weapons-grade — and increasingly delivered as sealed, factory-loaded cartridges that are never opened on site. Independent IAEA safeguards inspections track fuel from delivery to disposal.

Program names, locations, and statuses above are illustrative and rounded for a classroom overview — treat this as a starting map for further research, not a live status board. Nuclear programs and regulations move on timelines that shift often, and safety standards vary by country.
